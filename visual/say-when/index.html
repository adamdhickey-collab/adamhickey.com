Jargon, translated.

Specialty coffee is good at describing itself to people who already know. We write the bag for everyone else.

  • Washed Ethiopian, 1,950 masl, floral and citricBright. Toast with marmalade.
  • Natural process, notes of stone fruit and brown sugarRounder. Caramel and a walnut.
  • Water-process decaf, full body, cocoa finishDark chocolate, and you will still sleep.

How do you take it?

Three questions, none of them about altitude. Morning or evening, black or with milk, easy or bold. We point you at a bag and tell you why.

Find your when

Everyone has a when.

Say when is what you say when someone pours for you. It is the whole idea: the coffee stops where you want it to, and nobody tells you what you should have asked for.

So the roasts are named for the moments, not the farms. The line on every bag is the level in the cup. It sits low on the morning bag and high on the evening one, and the campaign is the same line drawn across the moments people actually have: 6:40 before the house wakes, the 2 p.m. you will get through, the cup you make for someone else.

It is a fictional brand, made to show how an idea carries from a name to a bag to a shop. The case study has the rest.

Say When is a self-initiated fictional brand concept by Adam Hickey, a senior digital designer in Minneapolis. There is no roaster, no shop and no stock. The storefront is a working prototype: the cart lives in your browser and the checkout is pretend.
