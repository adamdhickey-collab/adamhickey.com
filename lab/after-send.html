The invoice says paid. Why is the supplier still calling?

An independent concept on one question: what happens when a payment status is accurate, but doesn’t tell someone what they need to know? Here, the buyer issued a virtual card, and the supplier hasn’t charged it.

Why I built it: To show how I decide what the person taking the call needs to see first, then use AI to build the first working version in a day.

Try the prototype

Independent concept on simulated data. Wren Payments, the product, and every name and reference in it are made up.

The screen says paid. The supplier says unpaid.

  1. The phone rings

    You work in accounts payable at Maple Street Bakery, the buyer. Hillside Packaging, the supplier, calls: “Our records still show INV-20483 as unpaid, and your next order ships tomorrow.”

    You open INV-20483. Your system says paid. Now you have two different answers, and a person waiting on the phone.

  2. The card was sent. The money hasn’t moved.

    The buyer issued a virtual card: a one-time card number for the invoice amount.

    But the supplier still has to charge that card. Afterward, the buyer needs to match the charge to the invoice.

  3. “Paid” covers three separate steps

    A specialist who can’t tell which step is open has to guess: a follow-up nobody needed, or a second payment on top of a good one.

    The problem I chose: helping the specialist understand what happened, who acts next, and what to do now.

Three decisions replace “Paid” with an answer

  1. First decision

    Show the charge and the match separately

    I separated supplier processing from invoice matching, which makes the two remaining questions visible.

    Why: two facts can differ without either one lying. American Express vPayment also keeps the charge and the reconciliation as two events.

  2. Second decision

    Answer the call first, then the follow-up

    What to tell the supplier sits near the top, before the activity history.

    Why: the specialist is on a call.

  3. Third decision

    Show the supplier only what the buyer would share

    The supplier gets a focused notice with what it needs to process the payment. The buyer’s internal notes and accounting details stay with the buyer.

    Why: the handoff crosses two companies.

  4. What if the record doesn’t know?

    Payment information can arrive late, so a charge may already have happened.

    So the record shows when it last checked. For an expired card, it asks for a late-charge check before anyone replaces the payment. The one-day window is this scenario’s assumption.

Back to the call, with something to say

Same phone, same invoice. This time the specialist can say: “No charge posted as of 2:05 pm. The notice went to ar@hillsidepackaging.com. Is that the right inbox?”

From a log to an answer

I built the log first, and it couldn’t answer the supplier. You’re the buyer’s specialist, with the supplier on the phone. Both records come from the same events. Answer from each.

What I don’t know yet: will specialists trust the summary and still open the log when it matters?

The test I’d run first
  • The test: can specialists explain the status, say who acts next, and handle an expired payment without creating a duplicate? Half see each record first.
  • Measured: correct status readings, time to find the invoice, and any press that would duplicate a payment.
