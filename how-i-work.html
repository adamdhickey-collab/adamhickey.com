How I work as a product designer

What I do inside a product organization

Coding agents can build a working version in days, so the hard part is the decision on either side of the build: what should exist, and whether what was built is good enough. Five parts of the job, and the work that shows each.

Turn ambiguity into a decision

The product works, and nobody can say what it’s for anymore. Three people hold three accounts of the problem, and AI coding agents could build any of them by Friday. So I:

  • Hear everyone. Every person who owns a piece of it.
  • Name it. One sentence the room recognizes.
  • Settle the trade-off. What’s given up, agreed in the room.
  • Log the call. With the options turned down.
  • Make it real. Something concrete enough to judge.

Build the system people and agents build from

Four teams have built the same data table four ways. Their coding agents copy all four and make a fifth, without asking which is right. So I:

  • Find the repeats. What has been built twice.
  • Draw the line. What is shared, and what stays local.
  • Write what each part means. Where it applies, and where it doesn’t.
  • Put it in checks. Every change has to pass them.
  • Name the owners. Who can change it, written down.

Decide where a person stays in charge

The product is adding AI that recommends, then acts, and nobody has drawn the line between what it does alone and what it asks. So I:

  • Draw the line. Where a mistake gets expensive to undo.
  • Make the ask worth reading. The evidence, the consequence, and a way back.
  • Make no as cheap as yes. Or the approval is a formality.
  • Keep a record. Readable later, by someone who wasn’t there.

Agent Review · my own prototype, simulated

A question people answer every time stops being read.

In Relay, a made-up customer app, a simulated agent asks before archiving each batch of customers. In my simulated sessions, people click through its question by the third batch.

  • 3kinds of work an agent does, and two pause for a person

So it asks only when a batch holds an active customer, and names them. I haven’t tested it with anyone yet.

Build to find out

There are ideas, sketches, and a demo or three, and agents can build any of them in days. So I decide:

  • What comes first. The question that makes the next step risky.
  • What it may claim. No more than it can do.
  • Who tries it. Five people who do the work.
  • What happens next. Build it, kill it, or park it.

Own an initiative end to end

A funded initiative, staffed with engineers, stalls on design decisions no one is placed to make. I own its design from inside the team, including the parts nobody volunteers for:

  • The research. Who uses it, and what they need.
  • Every state of a screen. Empty, loading, error, and done, plus waiting, refused, and undone.
  • The edge cases. What happens when the data is wrong.
  • The code review. Design comments on every change, agents’ too.
