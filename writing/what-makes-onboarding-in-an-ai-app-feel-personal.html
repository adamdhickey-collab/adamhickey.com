What makes onboarding in an AI app feel personal?

Asking only what the AI will use, and showing it being used. A few questions about someone’s real constraints, like budget, time, and equipment, then the result built in front of them, turns a form into the first moment the product works for that person.

Three terms first

Onboarding
Everything between opening an app for the first time and getting something useful from it.
Constraint
A real limit in someone’s life, like time, money, or tools, that the answer has to fit.
First value
The moment a new person gets the thing they came for. Onboarding is the road to it.

Five questions about a life, not a profile

Josim Uddin designed the first run of Nutry, an AI nutrition app, as five short steps instead of a long form. Then the app builds the meal plan while the person watches.

Three of the five are about a life rather than a body: money, evenings, and what’s in the kitchen. Those are the answers a generic plan gets wrong.

Every question has to earn its place

Lucy Learns, my dog-training app, opens on today’s homework from the trainer, so the first screen already fits this dog.

Show the work at the end

I think the screen where Nutry builds the plan does the most work: it shows the answers going somewhere. Whether the plan fits is a separate test, and the post reports none.

Each answer turns a dial the person can watch move.

Personalizing needs data, and every question adds friction. Ask fewer, or ask each later, when its answer is used.

If an answer doesn’t change what the person sees, don’t ask the question.

Try it: trace each question

Optional, about twenty minutes. List what your product asks a new person, and trace each answer to what it changes.

See my map for Lucy Learns
  1. The homeworkThe trainer’s handout becomes today’s session, on the first screen.
  2. The levelsThe trainer’s levels become the program map, in her numbers.
  3. Each sessionThe results become the week, ready to hand to the trainer.

From Lucy Learns, a household app. This map covers what gets used. The exercise also asks how each answer is corrected and deleted.

Your turn, and how to tell it’s done
  1. List every question your product asks a new person.
  2. Beside each, write what the person sees change because of it.
  3. Cut or move any question with nothing beside it.
  4. For each one left, write how the person corrects it and deletes it.

It’s done when:

  • Every question changes something visible.
  • Each answer can be corrected where it’s used.
  • Each answer can be deleted.

Check yourself

1Which onboarding question in your product changes nothing the person sees?

Cut it, or move it to the moment its answer is actually used. Asked there, it explains itself.

2How does a new person know their answers were used?

If the result appears with no sign of the answers in it, the questions felt like a toll. Show the connection.

The idea: Josim Uddin, onboarding for Nutry, an AI nutrition app. The example: How I built Lucy Learns, on this site.
