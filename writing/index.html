Writing

Notes on making complicated software clearer: what a person needs the moment an operational workflow breaks, what an AI feature owes the person who has to act on it, how a team carries a change from an ambiguous request through to a release, and what usually sits behind an inconsistent product. Each one answers a question people ask before they know who to ask, in its first paragraph, from twenty years inside enterprise products and the tools that now build with them.

  1. Why enterprise UX problems are usually organizational problems

    When enterprise software is hard to use, the interface is where the problem showed up, not where it started. What sits behind inconsistency, stalled decisions and unused systems.

    5 min read

  2. Designing for the moment the workflow breaks

    When an order stalls or a transaction fails, people need more than an error message. They need to understand what happened, what it affects, and what they can safely do next.

    4 min read

  3. Enterprise AI should help people decide, not just answer

    A useful AI feature helps someone judge a recommendation and act on it appropriately. That means designing the evidence, the uncertainty, the controls, and the path when the suggestion is wrong.

    4 min read

  4. How I move a complex workflow from ambiguity to release

    A broad product request becomes useful through shared problem framing, focused investigation, realistic prototypes, and decisions made with engineering, not through a design handoff at the end.

    4 min read

  5. What should a working prototype actually prove?

    A prototype should resolve the uncertainty that makes the next investment risky, usually whether the idea is worth building: real code, real inputs, the people who would use it, and a build, kill, or park call.

    4 min read

  6. When does a product need a design system?

    A product needs a design system when several teams build the same component differently and the product shows it. How to tell, what belongs in one, and what keeps it alive.

    4 min read

  7. What I learned standardizing UX across 40+ SAP Fiori apps

    Lead UX for 40+ SAP Fiori apps built by different teams: one style guide and component library, feedback loops, checks in the release process, and teams that own it.

    4 min read

  8. Why AI-built interfaces drift from your design system

    Design systems had two audiences. AI coding tools are a third, and they generate from whatever the organization made legible. What an agent needs, and how to test for it.

    6 min read

  9. Where does design end and development begin, now?

    AI coding tools moved the line between design and development. Where it sits now: build, verify and stand behind, and when to bring an engineer in for leverage rather than rescue.

    6 min read
