What should you never do when building an agent?

Never trust a success the agent reports about itself. A call that returns “OK” may have done nothing, a confident answer may be wrong, and an action nobody can stop will eventually run when it shouldn’t. Most of the rules that prevent this are design decisions, not engineering ones.

Three terms first

Tool call
An agent asking another system to do something: send, save, charge, or look up.
Irreversible action
One a person can’t take back with a button: money moved, a message sent, a record deleted.
Kill switch
A way for a person to stop an agent mid-run, and know what it had already done.

A status can be accurate and still not tell you enough

In After Send, a concept on simulated data, a supplier calls a bakery’s accounts payable desk. Their records say the invoice is unpaid. The bakery’s screen says Paid.

Both are right. The bakery issued a one-time card, but the supplier hasn’t charged it. “Paid” covers three steps, and the screen collapsed them into one word.

Every rule on the list cost someone an incident

Alistair Greenwood wrote down 21 things he never does when building agents, each with a production incident behind it. The third is After Send’s problem in engineering terms: a successful response is not proof that anything happened.

The interface is where these rules become visible

Each rule needs a screen that keeps it. In After Send, the record shows when it last checked, and an expired card asks for a late-charge check before anyone pays twice.

A confidence score is a claim about the system. A source and an age are claims about the world, and a person knows what to do with those.

Stopping needs the same care. Undo, cancel, revert, and stop are four words and four promises, and a kill switch nobody can read is just a button.

Not all 21 are mine to design. Retries and regression gates belong to engineering. But I’d want to be in the room for the other half.

If the agent can say “done” when nothing happened, the screen has to say what actually did.

Check yourself

1Which status in your product could be accurate and still mislead someone?

Look for one word covering several steps. Each step that can be open on its own deserves its own line.

2If your agent had to be stopped right now, what would the person see?

If the answer is a spinner that goes away, the stop is designed for the system, not for the person who pressed it.
