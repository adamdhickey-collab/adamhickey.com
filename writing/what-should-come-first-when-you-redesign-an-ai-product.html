What comes first when you redesign an AI product?

Cut what it does before you redraw how it looks. Complexity first, then the core patterns, then the surface, because a fresh coat of paint on an AI product that does too much makes the excess look intentional. Polish is the last step of a redesign, not the first.

Three terms first

AI-native
Built around what an AI can do from the start, not an AI feature added to an older product.
Core pattern
The few interactions a product repeats everywhere: how you find, review, and act on things.
Building in public
Showing unfinished work as it happens, so the people it’s for can react early.

A CRM for one person starts by cutting

Chris Halaska is redesigning YouSpot, a CRM for one-person companies built with HubSpot’s co-founder, in public. It reads email, calendar, and LinkedIn to remember who you know and whom to follow up with.

Most CRMs are built for teams: pipelines, seats, handoffs, reporting. A founder needs almost none of that. He lists three priorities. The order below is mine, not his.

The pattern is where the agent meets the person

In Agent Review, a simulated story, an account manager archives three customers in Relay, a made-up app, and an active one goes with them. A coding agent built three working fixes in an afternoon’s work.

All three passed the checks. Choosing between them was a pattern decision, and no amount of visual polish would have made it for me.

Relationships raise the stakes on every pattern

Take closeness to a contact: set by the person, or inferred from their email? I think that’s the redesign’s real question: what the product may conclude about someone’s relationships on its own.

Decide what the product does before you decide how it looks. The order is the design.

Check yourself

1What does your product do that its smallest customer never needs?

That’s the first cut. Redesigning it first only makes it harder to remove later.

2Which conclusions does your AI draw about people without asking them?

Each is a pattern decision. Make it on purpose, and let the person see and change what was inferred.

The idea: Chris Halaska, redesigning YouSpot in public. The example: Agent Review, a simulated story, on this site.
