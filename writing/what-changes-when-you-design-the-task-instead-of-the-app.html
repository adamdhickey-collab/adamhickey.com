What changes when you design the task instead of the app?

The unit of design moves from one application’s screens to the whole job a person is trying to finish, with agents carrying the steps between tools. The design work becomes deciding what the agent gathers, what it shows, and where the person decides.

Three terms first

Agent
Software given a goal that takes several steps on its own, across tools, to reach it.
Agentic experience
Designing how people, agents, and software work together on one outcome. AX, for short.
Unit of design
What you draw the boundary around: a screen, an app, or now a task.

Nobody does their job in one app, so designing one app misses the job

A dispatcher assigning a load checks where each truck is, how many driving hours each driver has left, and what the customer was promised. Those answers usually live in different tools.

Designing each tool well still leaves the person carrying the job between them: copying, comparing, and remembering. That carrying is the part of the work nobody owns.

David Mahlmann calls this move from UX to AX. His example is a geoscientist working from data to models to simulations, across specialist tools that each keep doing their own job.

Start from the moment of the decision, not from a screen

When I built Dispatch Cockpit, a prototype on synthetic data, I started from the assignment. What does a dispatcher need in the second they choose a truck?

Not a better map. They need the evidence gathered and the rules already applied, so the choice is theirs and quick to defend.

When two trucks come out level, the cockpit says so. It declines to pick. I think that one sentence did more design work than any layout on the page.

Three tools, one job. Design the job, and the tools line up behind it.

The hard questions become questions about behavior

Once software carries the steps, the decisions that matter aren’t about where things sit. They’re about what the agent does at each step, and where it stops.

Screens still matter. A recommendation set badly gets ignored or, worse, trusted without a look. AX widens the job; it doesn’t retire the craft.

Design the job first. The screen is where the person checks it.

Check yourself

1In your product, what does a person still carry between tools?

That carrying is where an agent earns its place, and where the design work starts: what gets gathered, and what the person sees of it.

2Could a new teammate point to where the agent stops and the person decides?

If they can’t find it on the screen, the line exists only in the code, and nobody is really deciding.

The idea: David Mahlmann, From UX to AX: The Unit of Design Is Changing. The example: Dispatch Cockpit, on this site.
