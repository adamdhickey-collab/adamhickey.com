How do you run a design sprint for an AI product?

Keep the five days, and add the questions a classic sprint never had to ask: is the data there, what role does the AI play, where does its authority stop, and what would make a careful person trust it. Those decisions shape the prototype more than any sketch.

Three terms first

Design sprint
Five days from a problem to a prototype tested with real people: map, sketch, decide, prototype, test.
Autonomy boundary
The line between what the AI may do alone and what it has to ask a person about.
Feedback loop
How what people do with the AI’s output gets back in, to improve the next answer.

A classic sprint assumes the software does what it’s told

In a usual sprint, the prototype’s behavior is whatever the team drew. With AI in the product, behavior depends on data the team may not have, and it will sometimes be wrong.

Bansi Mehta shared the workbook her team at Koru UX uses with healthcare clients, where a wrong answer has a patient on the other end. It adds eight tools to the week.

Draw the boundary before anyone sketches

The two tools readers picked out were the autonomy boundary and the trust checklist. I’d run the boundary on day one, because every sketch after it depends on the answer.

In Dispatch Cockpit, my prototype on synthetic data, the line was set before the screen: it recommends a truck and never assigns one. The legal driving limit is checked before anything is ranked.

On Friday, test the wrong answer too

A sprint prototype usually shows the happy path. An AI prototype should also show a wrong recommendation, and the test counts how many people catch it.

The cockpit hasn’t been in front of a working dispatcher, so I can’t say how many would. That’s the session I’d run first.

If nobody in the test catches the wrong answer, the prototype taught them to stop looking.

Check yourself

1Could your team say, in one sentence, what the AI may do without asking?

If not, that’s the first hour of the sprint. Every sketch after it is guessing at the answer.

2Does your test plan include a moment where the AI is wrong on purpose?

Trust is only measured by what people do with a bad answer. A test of good answers measures how pleasant they are.

The idea: Bansi Mehta, Koru UX, AI design sprint workbook. The example: Dispatch Cockpit, on synthetic data, on this site.
