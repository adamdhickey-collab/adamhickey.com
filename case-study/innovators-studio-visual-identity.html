Case study · Internal brand and platform · 6 min read

An identity that made a global innovation network feel like one place

A company’s intrapreneurs were working on every continent, in a language only they spoke. One mark, one visual system and one home on the intranet turned that into a community with a front door, and a reason to walk through it.

Client
A global corporation’s market insights team
My part
Lead designer: identity, illustration, templates and the portal
With
A marketing manager and a production designer
When
Four fiscal years, through four program cohorts

01 · The challenge

Innovation was happening everywhere, and nobody could see it

The company had intrapreneurs on every continent. They ran pilots, built business cases and taught each other, and almost none of it was visible past the team doing it.

The work spoke in its own vocabulary: venture building, systematic innovation, business design. To everyone else it sounded like a club, and the people inside it couldn’t find each other either.

So the question I kept was a plain one. How do you turn a scattered effort into something people recognize at a glance and feel invited to join?

The answer had to work inside corporate brand guidelines, in PowerPoint and Outlook and SharePoint, and it had to say you belong here before anyone read a word.

02 · The idea

One word, stacked, with a fingerprint in it

The mark is the word STUDIO set in two rows of three, each letter its own green, and a fingerprint where the counter of the D would be. A studio is a place you go to make something. The fingerprint says the something is yours.

Six greens were the deliberate choice over one. The corporate palette gave us a green to live inside, and six steps of it let every piece of the system carry the brand without ever being the same green twice.

03 · Illustration and badges

Pictures of people, not diagrams of process

Innovation programs explain themselves with flowcharts. I drew scenes instead: a journey through a program as a road with stops on it, a rocket for the venture builders, a figure with a magnifying glass for the trend watchers.

The badges came out of the same drawings. Each program and each skill got one, and a badge you can earn is progress worth showing off, which is the one thing a dispersed community can’t get from a newsletter.

04 · The communication system

Templates that made anyone a credible storyteller

Once the look landed we translated the language. Innovation work drowns in its own vocabulary, so the templates did the hard part: a clear hierarchy, a steady pace, one idea per slide instead of five.

A presentation set, a newsletter and a podcast promo format, each with room for the speaker to make it theirs. The production designer built the masters; I set the system they followed.

05 · The digital home

A place to stay between launches

Once people showed up they needed somewhere to stay. The Studio portal, built on SharePoint, gave the work one home: news and member spotlights, a directory that leads with what someone knows, and an academy with a learning path for each role.

SharePoint is not a designer’s first choice. The job was making the identity survive it: the greens, the drawings and the badges carried into page layouts, cards and the belt levels of the certification program, inside what the platform would allow.

06 · What it did

An invisible network became a visible community

Four fiscal years of compounding programs, each cohort feeding the next and the Studio carrying the energy between launches. Intrapreneurs across geographies and functions started bringing their ideas through it, and the program team stopped having to explain what the Studio was.

  • RecognitionOne system across illustrations, badges and templates signaled belonging the moment anyone met the work.
  • EngagementBranded newsletters, decks and promos turned dry internal updates into pieces the network read and shared.
  • VelocityReusable templates and asset libraries cut campaign production time roughly in half, by the team’s own timesheets.
  • Network growthThe portal connected intrapreneurs across geographies and functions into a real community.
How I measured this

How the one measured figure above was counted, and what it cannot tell you.

Campaign production time, roughly half
The marketing team’s hours per campaign kit (the newsletter, the deck and the promo set) before and after the template library, from their project timesheets across a handful of campaigns. A small sample, so read it as a direction rather than a precise figure.
Recognition, engagement and community
Observed rather than measured: these describe what the program team saw and heard, and none of them carries a number.

What I did

  • Led the design: the mark, the palette, the illustration style and the badge system
  • Set the templates for presentations, newsletters, promos, one-pagers and business-model tools
  • Designed the portal, the directory and the academy inside SharePoint’s limits
  • Worked with a marketing manager and a production designer from the market insights team, who built the masters and ran the programs

What this page doesn’t claim

  • No adoption or engagement numbers beyond the one timesheet figure, which is a direction
  • The client and the programs are described without their names
  • The photographs in the banners are the company’s stock; the drawings, badges and layouts are the work
