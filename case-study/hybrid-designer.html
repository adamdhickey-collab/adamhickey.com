Studio years · 2005 to 2013 · 5 min read

Eight years of brand-led sites, campaigns and apps at three Minneapolis studios

Before enterprise software, I was an interactive designer and developer at ByDesign, LEVEL and Pixel Farm: websites, campaigns, identities and apps for brands that wanted to be felt rather than only read. These are the pieces I still show.

Where
ByDesign, LEVEL and Pixel Farm, Minneapolis
My part
Interactive designer and developer
When
2005 to 2013, the last five years at Pixel Farm
The work
Brand-led sites, digital campaigns, identities, iOS and iPad apps

01 · The studio

Making corporate ideas feel human

The internet was a noisy place in 2008, and it has only gotten louder. Everyone was shouting. Our job was to be the ones whispering something interesting in the corner: take a big corporate idea and make it feel like a person was telling it to you.

Sometimes we succeeded. Sometimes we just made really nice looking buttons. But we always tried to tell a story, and the story usually started with the pictures.

Designing and building weren’t separate jobs yet. I drew the site and then wrote the front end, and whoever shipped it sat close enough to argue with. That habit, of judging a design by the thing people could touch, is the one I kept.

02 · Brand-led sites

A site that feels like the brand, not a brochure about it

Two sites from the same years, for two very different brands, built on one conviction: the photography and the voice do the selling, and the interface should get out of their way while still being fun to use.

Why it looks like this

The texture is the argument

Lund sells a day on the water, so the site is one big photograph with the catalog underneath. Fulton sold the idea of ordinary guys brewing extraordinary beer, so everything is drawn by hand and set on paper, down to the taproom hours on a torn note. Neither site would work with the other’s skin on it.

03 · Campaigns and identity

One idea carried across every piece

A campaign only works if the website, the banners and the emails feel like one voice. The pieces here are the ones where the identity did the most work, including one I drew from the mark up.

04 · Apps and interfaces

Interfaces for a night, a century and a game

The apps were where the storytelling met the interface. Each had one job to do beautifully, on a screen somebody was holding in a crowd, a boardroom or a living room.

05 · What I took with me

The visual judgment came first

What I did

  • Designed and built interactive websites and apps across eight years and three studios, the last five at Pixel Farm
  • Took brand ideas from a client’s deck into sites and campaigns people wanted to spend time with
  • Drew one identity from the mark up, Learn to Live, with its illustration and its site
  • Worked beside the producers, writers, animators and developers who shipped each piece

What this page does not claim

  • These were studio projects with teams; the strategy, the photography and the video were other people’s crafts
  • No results are stated because none were measured in a way I can stand behind now
  • The captures are of the sites and apps as they shipped, from the studio’s own portfolio
