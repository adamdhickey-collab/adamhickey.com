How I’m building Faunaform

Seven Minnesota animals, each drawn for one hour of one day, to be screen printed by hand in four flat inks. An image model draws to my briefs and studio software holds every drawing to four exact inks. Then the AI stops, and I finish the drawing by hand in Illustrator. Nothing is printed yet; the first proof is next.

Everything: art direction, brand, the studio’s software, the shop’s design

The problem

An image model can draw a striking wildlife print in a minute. It can’t tell you whether the print will print, or whether the bear is a bear.

That gap is the art director’s job, and it always was: someone has to answer for every call.

The collection, One day of light, keeps one rule: each print takes its four inks from its own hour’s sky.

Who does what

Every step says who does it, which answers the question everyone asks about AI and art.

  • Me. The idea, every choice, the final drawing in Illustrator, and the printing.
  • An AI image model, ChatGPT. It draws from a written brief.
  • An AI assistant, Claude. It helps with research and turns my direction into written briefs.
  • Studio software. Ordinary code, not AI: it fixes the colors, marks what’s too thin, and makes the screens.

One print, start to finish

The black bear at golden hour, drawn from 16 to 20 October 2026.

Every print starts from something the animal really does here. Black bears feed by day and blueberries ripen in August, so the bear is in the berry patch, head down.

A black bear isn’t a small grizzly, so the brief spells out the marks: a straight face, no shoulder hump, tall round-tipped ears, a tan muzzle.

The fifth leg

Looking hard still misses things. I approved this bear on 19 October. Count its legs.

The brief had said “on all fours,” and the model drew five anyway. Saying it again wasn’t going to fix that. Four moves did, and they’re how I fix a drawing now.

The walking bear: Courtney Celley, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, in the public domain.

Measuring is what lets me trust an edit: software compares it with the drawing it came from, pixel by pixel. The paws pass changed 1.1% of the picture, all of it at the paws.

The model drew each take in about a minute. Seeing the fifth leg took me a day.

Making it printable

The model offers the options. I draw the final line.

Say what AI did, in plain words

“AI-assisted,” never “AI-generated,” and no render shown as finished. The label says where the AI stopped, because a fair or a buyer will ask.

How I’ll check it

  • The first proof. The bear, on paper and then on birch, with every rule confirmed or rewritten against it.
  • Buyers before a price. Two rounds of six to ten people.
  • Fairs asked first. Some here exclude AI-assisted work.

What it says about how I direct

It’s how I’d run any creative project: a standard every piece is checked against, a model to widen the search, and a person drawing what ships.
