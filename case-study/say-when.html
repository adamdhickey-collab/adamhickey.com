Self-initiated brand concept · 14 min read

Say When: a coffee brand built on three plain words.

Specialty coffee is very good at talking to people who already know. This is a brand for everyone else, carried from a name to a bag to a shop you can try. It is fictional, it has no client, and I made all of it.

What
Strategy, naming, identity, packaging, a campaign and a working storefront
For whom
No one. A self-initiated concept, with no client, no research study and nothing for sale
My part
All of it: the idea, the words, the drawings, the code
When
October 2026, in a week, with coding agents doing the typing

01 · The opportunity

Good coffee had learned to talk only to the initiated.

Walk a specialty shelf and the bags all speak the same second language: washed, natural, masl, stone fruit, a farm you cannot find on a map. It is accurate, and it is meant for people who already drink this way.

Everyone else buys the bag that looks friendly, or the one they bought last time. The category has sophistication to spare and very little hospitality.

So the brief I set myself was a full assignment, the kind a studio brings a prospective client: find the idea, build the visual language, carry it into packaging and a campaign, and finish with a shop that works. The constraint was honesty. Nothing here claims a client, a study, a print run or a sale, and where a real brand would need a photograph, the page says so rather than faking one.

02 · The insight

Nobody at the table asks about altitude. They say when.

The most generous thing a person does with coffee is pour it for someone else. The other person watches the cup and says one word, and the coffee stops exactly where they wanted it.

That is the whole relationship the category is missing. Expertise is on offer everywhere; being met where you are is not. Everyone's when is different: how much, how strong, with what, and at what hour. A brand that takes those as its questions can be as serious about the coffee as anyone, and never make the customer feel tested.

Say when is what you say when someone pours for you. The brand's job is to be the one pouring.

The sentence the rest was built on

03 · The creative idea

One line, drawn at the height you asked for.

Three territories went up before one came down. The first was the small hours, coffee as a private ritual before the day; it was calm and it was also every third coffee brand already. The second was plain speech, a brand that translates tasting notes into words you use; it was useful and a little cold. The third was hospitality, the pour and the person being poured for, and it turned out to hold the other two inside it.

The idea is a line. It is the level in the cup, and it sits where you said. On the bags it rises through the day, low on the morning roast and high on the evening one. In the campaign it runs under the moments people actually have. On the wordmark it stops in a short upright, the hand that says when.

  • The small hours

    Coffee as the quiet before the day. For people who guard the first twenty minutes.

    Lost: the calm-morning brand already exists several times over, and it tells people how to feel.

  • Plain speech

    A brand that writes the bag in your words. For people who find tasting notes a test they did not study for.

    Half kept: the translation is the right move, but on its own it is a feature, not a feeling.

  • Say when

    The pour, and the person being poured for. For anyone who has ever said it.

    Chosen: it is a gesture, it is generous, it names itself, and it gives the whole system one shape to draw.

The name came out of the territory rather than before it. Five were judged on memorability, personality, relevance, visual potential and obvious conflicts; none was checked legally, and this page does not claim any is clear.

  • PlainlySays the method, not the feeling. Reads as a software product, and the word is already in use as one.
  • Second CupThe second pour is the generous one, and the name is a well-known Canadian chain. Out on conflict alone.
  • CompanyCoffee as company, the company you keep. Warm, and too general to own a shelf or a search.
  • Kettle & KinHospitable, and already the shape of a dozen homeware brands. An ampersand is not an idea.
  • Say WhenA thing people already say, in the moment the brand is about. Two short words, a built-in gesture, and a line to draw. It is a common phrase, and cafes and bars may well use it; a real launch would start with a search.

04 · Identity development

Four ways to draw it, and the one that kept the gesture.

Four directions, each asked the same question: does it carry the pour? Every mark here is drawn by a script from a few numbers, with the type outlined, so the iterations are real files and not sketches of files.

A won, and then it was wrong four times in a row.

Why not an elaborate system

One good mark and one good line.

A brand this size does not need a family of marks. It needs the wordmark, the cup for the places words will not fit, and the line everywhere. The line is the system.

05 · The visual language

Paper, ink, and three grounds that rise through the day.

Six colours. Paper and ink do the talking; the three product grounds are the family, in the order the day goes; the red is spent once, on the level. Every pair that carries text was measured, and the tightest is ink on clay at 4.8 to 1, which is why the clay bag's small words are drawn, not typed.

  • Paper#f4ecdd. The ground.
  • Ink#2b2420. Espresso, not black. 13.0 on paper.
  • Butter#f0c45b. First Pour. Ink on it 9.3.
  • Clay#d4784f. Second Pour. Ink on it 4.8.
  • Plum#3b2d3e. Last Pour. Paper on it 11.0.
  • Red#b4382e. The level, once. 5.1 on paper.

The voice is the hardest part to specify and the easiest to hear. It describes, it does not instruct; it is specific about taste and vague about virtue; it is allowed one dry joke a page and no exclamation marks.

  • Ethically sourced, meticulously roasted, crafted for the discerning palate.Tastes like toast with marmalade.
  • Fuel your hustle. Seize the morning.6:40, before the house wakes.
  • Our decaf retains the full sensory profile of the original.Dark chocolate, and you will still sleep.

Motion follows the pour. There are three moves and they always happen in the same order, on this site's own durations.

The cup fills, from the bottom, to the line.
--motion-ambient, --ease-enter
The line draws itself and stops in the tick.
--motion-draw, --ease-enter
The words arrive, one rise.
--motion-enter, --motion-rise
A reader who asked for no motion sees the finished cup.
prefers-reduced-motion

Photography, which does not exist yet

Real pours, real tables, no beans.

The brief for a shoot is written and nothing has been shot: morning light on a kitchen counter, hands and cups, the pour mid-stream and a person about to speak. No burlap, no farms, no green hills. Until there are photographs the brand draws, and every picture on this page is drawn.

06 · Packaging

One pouch, three whens, and nothing on the bag you need explained.

A family is a system or it is three drawings. So the bag is one object, and each product changes exactly four things on it: the ground, the height of the line, the vessel standing on the line, and the words. The names are moments rather than origins, and the tasting line is the one you would say to a friend.

07 · The campaign

Everyone has a when.

The campaign sells no bag. It draws the line under the moments people already have, three of them to start, and lets the brand be the one that noticed. The same line, the same voice, a different hour each time. Outdoor, social, an email and the shop's own front page all carry it, and the bags carry it back.

The email is the campaign at its quietest: one moment, one line, one bag, and a button that says what it does. It is composed here in live type, so the site's own checks hold it to the same floors as the page around it.

The animated piece is the pour from the identity chapter, running on the shop's front door: the cup fills, the line stops, the words arrive. Thirty seconds of outdoor would be the same three moves with a person's hand in them, and that is a film nobody has shot.

08 · The digital experience

A shop that asks three questions instead of teaching a vocabulary.

The storefront is where the strategy has to work or it was only a poster. It is seven pages: a front door, the collection, three product pages, a finder and a checkout, with a cart that follows you between them. It is a working prototype on plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no back end and no payment; the cart lives in your browser and the checkout says it is pretend in its first line.

The discovery interaction is the finder. Three questions, none about altitude: when is the cup, how do you take it, how strong. Answer one and a pick appears; answer all three and it settles, and it tells you why in the words you gave it. It is the pour as an interface.

09 · Working interactions

Try it. The cart is yours and the checkout is pretend.

This is the storefront itself, live. Add a bag, open the cart, answer the finder, place the order that goes nowhere. Everything it saves stays in your own browser.

10 · What it shows

One idea, carried all the way, by one person who can.

The point of a self-initiated project is to show the whole arc without a brief to hide behind. The idea is small and it goes everywhere: a line, at the height you asked for, from the mark to the bag to the question the shop asks. Strategy chose it, design drew it, and code made it something you can use.

It is also the way I like to work: the words and the pictures and the build in one pair of hands, so the thing a creative director sees in the deck is the thing a customer gets on the screen.

What I did

  • The strategy, the territories and the name
  • The identity: four directions, four iterations, the lockups and the rules
  • The packaging family, the boxes and the shipper
  • The campaign idea and its pieces
  • The storefront: design, copy, markup, styles and the behaviour
  • The scripts that draw the artwork, so it is a system

What this is not

  • Not a client, a brief or an approval
  • Not research: the signals are a designer reading shelves and sites
  • Not a product: the roasts and their sourcing are a plausible concept, not coffee anyone has roasted
  • Not printed: every bag and box is a drawing, and the photography brief is unshot
  • Not a store: nothing is sold, charged or shipped, and the name has not been cleared
