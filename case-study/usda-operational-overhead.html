Case study · 5 min read

Turning a nutrition network’s scattered grant tools into one platform people trust

Spreadsheets replaced by a portal, and a 45-to-55-hour process down to just over ten.

Client
Gretchen Swanson Center for Nutrition, for the USDA-backed Nutrition Incentive Hub
My role
Lead UX design consultant
Timeline
8 months

Overview

Nutrition Incentive Hub

A USDA-backed coalition supporting nutrition incentive and produce prescription projects, which put healthy food on the tables of families who need it most.

The problem

The network ran on old software, spreadsheets, and workarounds, and no two of them agreed. Grantees spent more time chasing paperwork than running programs, and administrators couldn’t tell which programs were working.

The solution

I led the design of one platform to replace all of it: a public website, a portal where grantees apply and report, and the Power BI reports administrators read. One record carries each grant from application to analysis, and grantees and administrators could finally trust what they found.

My part

I led the research, information architecture, visual design, and design system across the four roles in the map below, and checked each pattern against WCAG 2.1 AA. Developers built the website, the portal, and the Power BI reports, and the center’s staff decided what came first.

My approach

1Research

We paired a system audit with interviews and journey maps. The data showed what was slow, the interviews showed what was stressful, and they rarely pointed at the same thing.

System audit & analysis

What we discovered

  • Found where the spreadsheets slowed everyone down
  • Matched federal reporting rules to what the system could do
  • Documented every tool in use, to replace the paper trail

Stakeholder inquiry

The human truth

  • Interviewed users to find what wore them down
  • Made the case for one live portal
  • Found where people got lost while applying

Persona development

Building empathy

  • Built personas around how people use the system
  • Named the groups having the hardest time
  • Gave developers real people to build for

Journey mapping

Mapping pain points

  • Mapped the trip from applying to getting funded
  • Marked the moments people got confused
  • Added help where people needed it most

Human judgment

A job title doesn’t tell you what someone needs from a screen. What they came to do does.

The client started with far too many personas, one per job title, across both the website and the portal. I pushed back. We narrowed it to a handful based on how people use the system, such as collaborators, reporters, and reviewers.

2Information architecture

We organized the content around how people look for things, so a first-time grantee could find what they needed without knowing where it lived.

The user flows traced each handoff, form to screen: what a grantee enters in a report is what an analyst checks, so validation moved into the form, and fewer errors reached the analyst at all.

Strategic content org

Making complexity simple

  • Renamed things the way people actually say them
  • Put every resource within two clicks
  • Built room for the network to keep growing

Workflow optimization

Removing friction

  • Drew the user flows behind every decision path
  • Traced each grantee form to the reviewer screen it feeds

3Visual design

Public-sector tools often feel cold. We aimed for the authority a federal program needs and the warmth its community deserves.

Conceptual exploration

Finding the right balance

  • Balanced federal authority with a human feel
  • Tried directions that met the rules and still felt usable

Prototyping & validation

De-risking the design

  • Tested assumptions on rough screens first
  • Applied WCAG standards from the first draft
  • Filled out the forms ourselves to find the errors

4User interface

The last layer made it last: a design system and component library, every pattern checked against WCAG 2.1 AA, so the platform could grow without coming apart again.

Digital design system

The foundation

  • Built one grid that works on any device
  • Built accessibility into the foundation

Component library

Built to last

  • One place designers and developers both work from
  • Small components that combine into new screens

The impact

Today the platform carries 135+ grantees and 200+ grants across the U.S., with 200,000+ page requests a week and 450,000+ metrics captured a year.

How I measured this

How each figure in the map and the line above was counted, over what period, and what it cannot tell you.
