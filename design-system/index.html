Principles

Four ideas decide most questions before they are asked. When a choice is not covered below, one of these usually settles it.

  • One accent, chosen by the groundWarm grounds, dark ink and a single sage. Neutrals do the layout work. Which sage you use depends on what it sits on, never on the job it is doing.
  • Size or the accent, never bothThe serif carries headings at regular weight, and hierarchy comes from size. Montserrat carries labels and interface. Emphasis gets one signal, not two.
  • Motion confirms, directs or sets a placeContent rises once as it enters, then holds still. An animation a reader would not miss is decoration, and decoration is cut.
  • Measured, not assertedEvery value on this page is read from the stylesheet as it loads, and every page of the site is checked against these rules on each merge. See How it’s enforced.

Color

Warm grounds, dark ink and one sage. The sage changes by ground, not by job: one value on every light ground, for text and fills alike, and one on charcoal. Its hover is a darker step of the same color, shown with it below rather than as a separate swatch. COLOR.md is normative for all of it.

  • WarmThe warm ground, and the primary ink on charcoal.

Artwork is not interface

The palette governs interface color, and the boundary is deliberate. An illustration is allowed its own palette, and a rendering of a physical object is allowed to look like the object. All three specs draw the same line: COLOR.md section 6 for color, SPACING.md section 4 for the space steps, MOTION.md section 6 for durations.

  • The story scene has colors of its own--story-bg, --story-ink, --story-ink-soft, --story-body, --story-rail and --story-accent, scoped to the story section in style.css. Snapping them onto the palette would flatten the scene into one ground, which is the opposite of the point. They are named, scoped and deliberate.
  • So is the device chrome--device-chrome is off the palette for the same reason, and lives in style.css rather than color.css so that naming it cannot quietly make it palette. See Media below.
  • “Artwork” is a claim about the thing, not an excuseThe test is whether it renders something that exists outside the interface. A card is not artwork because its color is inconvenient. And the exemption is from the scale, never from legibility: text over an illustration still has to clear its floor, measured in pixels where a token cannot answer.

03

Type

Two families split by job: Crimson Text at regular weight for anything that speaks, Montserrat for anything that labels. Headings never take bold. Hierarchy comes from size, and the serif’s own contrast does the rest. Every size below is a step on one scale, and each specimen renders at the live token and reports the pixel value the browser computed, so a step that drifts shows here first. TYPOGRAPHY.md is normative for all of it.

On the words. A dek is the short line between the headline and the body, and a lede is the opening passage of the body. Both are American newsroom spellings, misspelled on purpose so an editor’s note could never be typeset as copy, in the same tradition as hed and graf. They are the terms the class names use. The full list is in TYPOGRAPHY.md §1.

The scale

Fourteen steps, base 16px, 11px to 80px. Two tiers on two different principles. The text tier steps arithmetically, because at small sizes the eye reads the absolute difference and not the ratio. The display tier is geometric, and the rule that generates it is worth memorizing: every step doubles four steps later. 20 → 40, 24 → 48, 28 → 56, 34 → 68, 40 → 80. That is a ratio of 21/4, about 1.189, rounded to whole pixels. You can extend it in either direction without a calculator: halve or double, then fill the three between.

Not invented for this page. Of the 587 type-size declarations the site carried when the scale was set, 78% already landed exactly on one of these steps and 95% within a pixel. The scale is the site’s own center of gravity with the rounding noise taken out.

You never hand-write clamp(). There are four ramps, each interpolating between two named steps, and all four share one viewport window: 400px to 1280px. Nothing below h3 is fluid: a 20px label is 20px on every screen. The last column is live: resize this window and watch it move.

A ramp is sized against the column it lands in, not against the page.

04

Spacing

Every space on this site is a multiple of 4px. That was already true of 78.5% of it before a single token existed, so the grid was finished here rather than introduced. Below 48px, space is fixed and comes off the nine steps. Above it, space is fluid and comes off a ramp, for the same reason type does. The bars render at their live token and report the pixel value the browser computed, so a step that drifts shows here first. SPACING.md is normative for all of it.

The nine steps

Two tiers, and the change of tier is the point. 4 to 24 in steps of 4, because at 8px a 4px error is half the value. 24 to 48 in steps of 8, because at 40px it is 10% and invisible, and a step nobody can see is a step nobody should have to choose between. The Tailwind column is not a coincidence: the eight most-used numeric utilities across the six case studies are eight of these nine, in rank order, and the config now reads them from the same tokens.

The measure

A line of text is measured in characters, not pixels. Comfortable is 60 to 75, and --measure is 52ch, which lays out about 70. It is in ch rather than rem because a rem measure can only be right at one size, and this site sets body text at five.

This paragraph is capped at --measure. One ch is 1.349 average characters in Montserrat and 1.324 in Crimson Text, measured in Chromium against a real paragraph, so a single token serves both families and stays correct at every size.

Apply it to the text element, never to a wrapper: ch resolves against the element’s own font-size, so a 16px container capping an 18px paragraph would measure the wrong glyph.

The measure caps the text, not the column that holds it. .case-narrow is 896px and stays there, because the figures, fact lists and step cards inside it want the room: capping the container instead would shrink every figure on the build write-ups by 23%. The engagement pages take .case-wide, the same column at the full 1280px frame, because a step card needs about 1200px to give its text the measure beside a readable illustration.

Corners

A radius is a length, so it answers to the 4px grid like any other. Two tokens name the two jobs a corner does in the interface, and a third belongs to the artwork. SPACING.md is normative.

  • --radius-pillA control whose corners are its short side: a tag, the back button, a progress track.
  • --radius-xlA panel: a card, a thumbnail, a framed figure, and every specimen on this page.
  • 50%circleA shape rather than a corner: a portrait, the round video wrap. Outside the scale.
  • A device shell takes its own radius--shell-radius is set per device, at 18, 26 or 30px, because a phone frame is a drawing of an object. Its screen derives its corner with calc(), outer radius minus bezel, so the frame reads as a ring of one width.
  • Focus rings sit outside what they ringThe ring draws at 4px with a 3px offset, so it frames the control rather than matching its corner.
  • Two button shapes, by jobThe primary and ghost buttons are square. The back button is a pill, the shape this rule gives any control whose corners are its short side.

06

Motion

Motion here confirms, directs, or sets a place. Confirm: something the reader did had an effect. Direct: something arrived and is worth looking at. Set a place: the story drifts through four grounds while nobody watches. Anything that is none of those three is decoration, and the test for a new animation is whether a reader who missed it would have lost something. MOTION.md is normative for all of it.

The six durations

The name is the job, not the number. The gaps are perceptual rather than tidy: 80 to 200 is the difference between tracking the reader and responding to them, 200 to 300 between a color changing and a thing that moved, 500 to 900 between arriving and being drawn. Each dot below runs on its own live token and reports what the browser computed.

Six speeds, side by side

  • --motion-response

    The interface tracking the reader in real time: the scroll-progress bar, a pointer follow. Anything slower reads as lag rather than as motion.

  • --motion-state

    A property changing under the pointer or the keyboard: color, opacity, background. The most common motion on the site.

  • --motion-move

    Something shifting a short distance or lifting: a card on hover, a shadow deepening.

  • --motion-enter

    An element arriving: a reveal, a thumbnail settling, a step becoming active.

  • --motion-draw

    A line describing itself. SVG stroke animations only: a drawing takes the time a drawing takes.

  • --motion-ambient

    The environment changing behind the content: the story ground drifting between beats. Not meant to be watched.

The two curves

Same distance, same duration. Only the shape of the acceleration differs, which is the only way to see why there are two. --ease is the default for everything that is not entering. --ease-enter decelerates hard, so an arriving element reads as caught rather than stopped.

.reveal rises --motion-rise (20px) and fades in once as it enters the viewport, over --motion-enter on --ease-enter. Siblings offset by --motion-stagger (60ms), multiplied by index. Two more distances complete the vocabulary: --motion-lift (1.03) is how much a hoverable thing grows, and --motion-press (0.985) how much a pressed thing shrinks. A 3% shrink reads as a flinch on a large surface, which is why it is not 0.97. The hidden state is gated behind a .js class, so without JavaScript the page renders complete rather than blank: an animation that reveals content is never the only thing that reveals it.

Reduced motion

Reading your setting…

A reader who asks for reduced motion sees no motion and loses no content. Both halves matter. One blanket rule in style.css neuters every CSS animation and transition on the site without naming any of them, and both JavaScript motion files check the query and return before running. So a new animation needs no reduced-motion rule of its own. Add one only when the element’s un-animated resting state is wrong, and say why in a comment. The lightbox is the one deliberate exception: it keeps a 150ms linear fade rather than snapping, because an overlay that appears between one frame and the next is more disorienting than one that takes an eighth of a second.

Disclosure

Why I designed it this way

A ranking is a summary of reasons. Hide them and the reader has to rebuild them to check it, which is more work than not having been given the ranking at all.

The row is the component, and the rest belongs to whoever uses it. .disclosure-row in style.css is a full-width row with the chevron leading its label, the mark pointing at the content when closed and turning down into it when open. What the row does not carry is a hairline above it, a width or a type size: those belong to the page that uses it. The chevron is 1em wide and one line tall, so it scales with its label and stays on the first line when the label wraps.

Media

Two clips run on this site: the header logo reveal and the hero portrait. Both follow one rule, learned the hard way.

Device chrome

A phone mockup is a drawing of a physical object, so its frame answers to the artwork rule rather than to the scales. SPACING.md section 4 carves it out of the space steps, and COLOR.md section 6 carves its color out of the palette. Changing how much room a card gives its contents must never change how a phone looks. Both tokens live in style.css, not shell.css, for exactly that reason.

  • --bezel: 8px, one thicknessEvery device frame is one thickness. A frame that changes with the device it draws reads as drift, not as a ramp.
  • --device-chrome: #1b1d22A shade darker and a few degrees cooler than the charcoal, which is what lets a shell read as a molded object beside charcoal type. The two are 1.10:1 apart, close enough that only a token keeps them apart.
  • The screen’s corner is derived, never authoredA frame reads as a constant-width ring only when the screen’s corner is concentric with the shell’s: inner radius equals outer minus bezel. Each device declares its own --shell-radius, and the screen derives its corner with calc(), so the relationship cannot drift.
  • A clip that plays once hands the element backWhen the reveal finishes it fades out over the static mark and removes itself. Leaving a paused video holding a required element looks fine until the page is backgrounded: browsers are free to drop a paused video’s decoded frame, and it returns empty. That is a real bug this site shipped and fixed.
  • The static state sets the boxThe mark or poster stays in flow and defines the space; the clip is layered over it, and the mark is painted beneath from the clip’s first frame. The header never shifts, the fallback is already there rather than swapped in late, and the hand-back is a fade between two things on screen, not a swap that waits on a phone to rasterize the SVG.
  • Every clip has a deadlineA start deadline for one that never begins, an end handler for one that finishes, and a backstop for one that stalls in between. All three land on the same finished state.
  • Silent, and never requiredMuted, playsinline, and skipped entirely under reduced motion. Nothing the clip carries is information the page needs.

01

How it’s enforced

A design system is only as true as the last time someone checked it. Ten scripts check this one. They read the four specs as rules and every rendered page as evidence, run after every merge across all thirty-two pages, and open an issue naming the commit when one fails. Any of them can also run against a branch before a risky merge.

In about a second, with nothing installed

  • deployable.mjsThe tree can ship: no tracked symlink or ignored file that would break the deploy.
  • tokens.mjsEvery token the specs and this page name exists in the CSS, and every swatch here resolves to a declared value.
  • counts.mjsEvery number the docs assert is recounted from the files on disk, including the page count in the sentence above.
  • seo.mjsEach page’s canonical address, share card and structured data agree with the page, and the sitemap lists every page once.
  • keys.mjsEach article’s key ideas are still its section headings.

In a browser, on every page

  • resting.mjsEvery color a reader can see clears its contrast floor at rest, measured by the pixel where text sits on a photograph.
  • states.mjsEvery hover, focus and script-applied state clears the same floors, including states a page only reaches after a press.
  • typescale.mjsEvery rendered type size is a step on the scale, at four widths from 375 to 1440px.
  • curves.mjsNo border stops partway around a rounded corner.
  • cards.mjsEvery card on a ground has an edge that reads, at 1.2:1 or better.

The Tailwind build is also rebuilt from source on every run and compared byte for byte with the committed file. Each check prints the page count and the totals it measured before its verdict, because a check that quietly measures less is worse than one that fails: it passes while looking at less.

02

Changing it

Rules earned the hard way. Each of these exists because skipping it broke something real.

  • Every asset change bumps its tokenStylesheets, scripts, images, the favicon, the clips: all carry ?v=. A returning visitor holds the old file otherwise, and the bug that follows is invisible to whoever shipped it, because their own browser fetched the new one.
  • There is no include stepThe header and footer are hand-written into every page. A change to either is a change to every file that carries it. Grep, do not assume, and prefer shared JavaScript over pasted markup for anything with behavior.
  • Measure, do not eyeballRead the number back out of the browser: computed styles, bounding boxes, contrast ratios, line counts. Twice this year a change looked right and was not, and both times the measurement caught it before it shipped.
  • Retire, do not accumulateWhen two tokens differ only where a rule forced them apart, the rule is the thing to fix. Two colors left this palette that way, and both had been quietly failing contrast at small sizes.
  • The reference is only true if it ships with the changeA specimen copied by hand goes stale the moment the site moves. Three tags on this page said four for a week. Update the section in the same pass as the code, or it becomes fiction.
03

Decision log

The reference above says what to use. This is how each part got there: what the audits found, and what changed. It is kept because the reasons are what stop a rule from being quietly undone.

ColorEighty hand-written values, brought onto the palette

The first color audit found 80 distinct values against 11 tokens, 26 hex and 54 rgba(), and only 21% of color uses going through a token. Most of the rest turned out not to be in the stylesheets at all: 319 were SVG attributes inside the illustration scenes, which the artwork rule exempts, and 486 were in the six Tailwind case studies.

  • A token that was never defined. site-nav.css asked for --color-accent five times. It did not exist, so all five had always rendered through a literal fallback that happened to look right.
  • A retired color that never left. The raw sage was still in nine places, one of them a token with no consumers. Eight became --color-accent-ring, written as color-mix() so it cannot drift again, and the dead token was deleted.
  • Two accessibility failures, both in the lightbox. An 11px hint at 3.62:1 and a close button whose only boundary measured 1.93:1. Both were fixed, and the accessibility rules stopped claiming 4.5:1 everywhere until it was true.
  • Six near-blacks and their near-duplicates collapsed onto black, one scrim token, white and charcoal. Every off-palette value left is a documented exception.
  • The Tailwind color list, copied six times, became one set of var(--color-*) references: 510 utility replacements, and zero rendered colors changed.

Ranking near-duplicates by luminance got two of them wrong, including a slate blue matched to the sage because the two are equally light. Luminance answers how light a color is, which is right for contrast and wrong for whether two colors are the same.

ColorFour sages became three

The sage on light was two values: #556b51 for text and #657d60 for fills, on the theory that fills wanted a lighter step. Nobody had checked. The site’s largest sage fill, the primary button, had used the text step all along, because warm type on the lighter sage measured 4.12:1, under the text floor.

Merging the two put every fill further from its ground, and turned up the one place it could not be a straight swap: the focus ring on charcoal, where the text sage reads 2.63:1. The ring became a token each ground sets, and on charcoal it went from 3.40:1 to 7.47:1.

ButtonsA hover nobody could see

The state grid on this page showed it the day it was built: none of the three buttons changed visibly under a pointer.

  • The primary mixed the sage 90% with transparent, letting the ground through, so it moved 1.25:1 from rest. On white its warm label fell to 4.24:1, under the text floor, and no check saw it, because a color-mix() with transparency is a color the contrast checks cannot composite. It takes --color-accent-hover now: 6.90:1, and a change you can see.
  • The ghost filled charcoal on hover, which on the dark band is no change at all. Every ghost on the site turned out to be the light-ground form, whose hover already inverted, so the dark form takes the dark band’s hover wash.
  • The back pill had a transition and no hover rule. It takes the light-ground ghost’s, so the two outline buttons answer a pointer the same way.

The same pass found this page documenting the buttons on the wrong ground: primary and ghost live on light grounds, and on the dark band the primary turns warm.

AlphaTwenty alphas onto seven

Warm carried fourteen distinct alphas on charcoal, six under 2:1 doing the same decorative job and five above 4.5:1 doing the same text job, and charcoal six more. Both collapsed onto the seven in the Alpha table. 39 elements changed color, and every one still passes AA. Applying it turned up a role with no token, a surface tint, so the washes exist now.
