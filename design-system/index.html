Principles

Four ideas decide most questions before they are asked. When a choice is not covered below, one of these usually settles it.

  • One accent, chosen by the groundWarm grounds, dark ink, and a single sage. Neutrals do the layout work. Which sage you use depends on what it sits on, never on the job it is doing.
  • Size or the accent, never bothThe serif carries headings at regular weight, and hierarchy comes from size. Montserrat carries labels and interface. Emphasis gets one signal, not two.
  • Motion confirms, directs, or sets a placeContent rises once as it enters, then holds still. An animation a reader would not miss is decoration, and decoration is cut.
  • Measured, not assertedEvery value on this page is read from the stylesheet as it loads, and every page of the site is checked against these rules on each merge. See How it’s enforced.

Color

Warm grounds, dark ink, and one sage. The sage changes by ground, not by job: one value on every light ground, for text and fills alike, and one on charcoal. Its hover is a darker step, shown beside it.

Neutrals · grounds and ink

  • WhiteThe page ground for case studies and build write-ups.
  • WarmThe warm ground, and the primary ink on charcoal.
  • TeaThe feature ground: a hero card, a pull-out, anything that deserves a moment.
  • Muted lightThe alternate ground, for separating neighbors without changing temperature.
  • Muted graySecondary ink on the light grounds. Never on charcoal, where it reads 2.37:1.
  • CharcoalPrimary ink on every light ground, and the dark ground itself.

Accent · one sage, two grounds

  • Default
    Hover
    Sage, on lightText and fill both: links, eyebrows, kickers, button grounds, borders, icons. Clears 4.5:1 on all four light grounds, 4.60 at worst. Hover takes the darker step, so a press reads as a press.
  • Sage, on darkThe sage on charcoal, at 7.47:1, for ink and for the focus ring. The sage on light reads 2.63:1 there.

Status · caution

  • Caution inkCaution text on the light grounds, 5.09:1 at worst. Carries warm type as a filled status line, at 5.90:1.
  • Caution groundA close call, a factor that hurts. Charcoal on it reads 12.44:1 and the caution ink 5.23:1.
  • Caution, on darkLine-art icons on charcoal only, at 4.17:1. Never on a light ground, where it reads 2.62:1.

4.50+ passes AA for text at any size. 3.00+ is legal for text at 24px and up, and for the boundary of a control. under 3 is decoration only.

  • The four light grounds share one ink systemCharcoal, muted gray, and the sage are all legal as body text on white, warm, tea, and muted light. That is the whole reason four grounds are allowed to exist: four surfaces, one set of inks, nothing to remember.
  • Charcoal flips completelyMuted gray drops to 2.37:1 on it and the light sage to 2.63:1, so neither appears there as ink. The dark band takes warm and the sage on dark. A fill is a different question from an ink, because what has to clear the floor is the type on it. On the dark band the primary button is a warm fill with charcoal type. This is the most plausible mistake in the system, because muted gray is the right answer on all four other grounds.
  • Sage on muted light has no headroom4.60:1 against a 4.5 floor. It passes, and it is the first pair to re-measure if either value ever moves.

The scale

Fourteen steps, 11px to 80px. The text tier steps arithmetically, because at small sizes the eye reads the absolute difference. The display tier is geometric, a ratio of 21/4: every step doubles four steps later, so 20 becomes 40 and 40 becomes 80.

Not invented for this page: of the 587 type-size declarations the site carried when the scale was set, 78% already landed on one of these steps and 95% within a pixel.

Spacing

Every space on this site is a multiple of 4px, which 78.5% of it already was before a single token existed. Below 48px, space is fixed and comes off the nine steps. Above it, space is fluid and comes off a ramp.

The nine steps

Two tiers. 4 to 24 in steps of 4, because at 8px a 4px error is half the value. 24 to 48 in steps of 8, because at 40px it is invisible. The Tailwind config reads the same tokens.

  • --space-2xs p-1

    Hairline separation. A label off its icon; a tag off its neighbor.

  • --space-xs p-2

    The tightest real gap. Inside a pill, between stacked metadata lines.

  • --space-sm p-3

    A dense inset, a tight row gap.

  • --space-md p-4

    The default. When no reason says otherwise, this.

  • --space-lg p-5

    A comfortable inset for a small card.

  • --space-xl p-6

    The most-used space on the site. Card padding, grid gap, the standard stack.

  • --space-2xl p-8

    Between components. A card off the next card.

  • --space-3xl p-10

    A generous inset for a large card or a panel.

  • --space-4xl p-12

    The largest fixed step. Beyond this, a ramp.

The four ramps

The space ramps share the type ramps’ window, 400px to 1280px. They do not keep a constant ratio between type and space: h1 doubles across the window while --space-section grows by half, so the page reads denser at 1280 than at 400.

The measure

A line of text is measured in characters, not pixels. --measure is 52ch, which lays out about 70. It is in ch because a rem measure is right at only one size, and this site sets body text at five.

This paragraph is capped at --measure. One ch is 1.349 average characters in Montserrat and 1.324 in Crimson Text, measured in Chromium against a real paragraph, so a single token serves both families and stays correct at every size.

Apply it to the text element, never to a wrapper: ch resolves against the element’s own font-size, so a 16px container capping an 18px paragraph would measure the wrong glyph.

The measure caps the text, not the column. .case-narrow stays 896px because its figures and fact lists want the room, and the engagement pages take .case-wide, the same column at the full 1280px frame.

Corners

A radius is a length, so it answers to the 4px grid. Two tokens name the two jobs a corner does in the interface, and a third belongs to the artwork.

  • --radius-pillA control whose corners are its short side: a tag, the back button, a progress track.
  • --radius-xlA panel: a card, a thumbnail, a framed figure, and every specimen on this page.
  • 50%circleA shape rather than a corner: a portrait, the round video wrap. Outside the scale.
  • A device shell takes its own radius--shell-radius is set per device, at 18, 26, or 30px, because a phone frame is a drawing of an object. Its screen derives its corner with calc(), outer radius minus bezel, so the frame reads as a ring of one width.
  • Focus rings sit outside what they ringThe ring draws at 4px with a 3px offset, so it frames the control rather than matching its corner.
  • Two button shapes, by jobThe primary and ghost buttons are square. The back button is a pill, the shape this rule gives any control whose corners are its short side.

Breakpoints

Four. Three are Tailwind’s, which the six Tailwind case studies already used, and the fourth is earned. New work reaches for these before adding another.

06

Motion

Motion here confirms, directs, or sets a place. Confirm: something the reader did had an effect. Direct: something arrived and is worth looking at. Set a place: the story drifts through four grounds while nobody watches. Anything else is decoration.

The six durations

The name is the job, not the number. The gaps are perceptual: 80 to 200 is tracking the reader against responding, 200 to 300 a color changing against a thing that moved, 500 to 900 arriving against being drawn.

Six speeds, side by side

  • --motion-response

    The interface tracking the reader in real time: the scroll-progress bar, a pointer follow. Anything slower reads as lag rather than as motion.

  • --motion-state

    A property changing under the pointer or the keyboard: color, opacity, background. The most common motion on the site.

  • --motion-move

    Something shifting a short distance or lifting: a card on hover, a shadow deepening.

  • --motion-enter

    An element arriving: a reveal, a thumbnail settling, a step becoming active.

  • --motion-draw

    A line describing itself. SVG stroke animations only: a drawing takes the time a drawing takes.

  • --motion-ambient

    The environment changing behind the content: the story ground drifting between beats. Not meant to be watched.

The two curves

Same distance, same duration, different acceleration. --ease is the default for everything that is not entering. --ease-enter decelerates hard, so an arriving element reads as caught rather than stopped.

A third is written out rather than tokenized: linear, for progress bars and cross-fades, where there is nothing to accelerate. Reaching for it should be a decision.

Disclosure

Why I designed it this way

A ranking is a summary of reasons. Hide them and the reader has to rebuild them to check it, which is more work than not having been given the ranking at all.

The row is the component, and the rest belongs to whoever uses it. .disclosure-row puts the chevron before its label, turning down when open. The hairline, width, and type size belong to the page. The chevron is 1em wide, so it scales with its label.

Icons

All icons are line art in the accent color, drawn on as they enter. One grid: a 24 viewBox, a 1.5 stroke, round caps and joins, and pathLength="1" on every shape.

  • Stroke only, never fillstroke="currentColor", so the icon takes its color from context: the sage on dark over charcoal, the sage on light everywhere else.
  • Every shape carries pathLengthOne normalized length means one stroke-dasharray rule draws every icon on uniformly, whatever its real geometry. The rule animates path and circle, so draw a line or a rectangle as a path.
  • Decorative, alwaysaria-hidden="true" on every icon. The label beside it carries the meaning, and the icon never carries it alone.
  • Two to five shapesPast five strokes an icon stops reading as a mark at the sizes the site sets it. Simplify the idea before adding a line.

03

Media

Two clips run on this site: the header logo reveal and the hero portrait. Both follow one rule, learned the hard way.

  • A clip that plays once hands the element backWhen the reveal finishes it fades out over the static mark and removes itself. Leaving a paused video holding a required element looks fine until the page is backgrounded: browsers are free to drop a paused video’s decoded frame, and it returns empty. That is a real bug this site shipped and fixed.
  • The static state sets the boxThe mark or poster stays in flow and defines the space; the clip is layered over it, and the mark is painted beneath from the clip’s first frame. The header never shifts, the fallback is already there rather than swapped in late, and the hand-back is a fade between two things on screen, not a swap that waits on a phone to rasterize the SVG.
  • Every clip has a deadlineA start deadline for one that never begins, an end handler for one that finishes, and a backstop for one that stalls in between. All three land on the same finished state.
  • Silent, and never requiredMuted, playsinline, and skipped entirely under reduced motion. Nothing the clip carries is information the page needs.

Accessibility

Not a checklist pass at the end; these are standing patterns every page ships with.

  • A skip link on every pageFirst focusable element, off-screen until focused, jumps to #main. Its focused state:
Skip to main content
  • One focus ring, everywhereEvery link and button: a 2px outline in --color-focus-ring, offset 3px, on :focus-visible, so keyboard users get it and mouse clicks stay clean. Each ground sets the ring’s color, so on charcoal it is the sage on dark, as below.
Start a conversation
  • Reduced motion is a contractEvery animation carries a prefers-reduced-motion path that lands on the finished state. The custom cursor opts out entirely and leaves the native one.
  • No JavaScript, no blank pageHidden-until-revealed states are gated behind a .js class the script sets. When the script never runs, everything simply renders complete.
  • Decoration declares itselfIcons and ornamental media carry aria-hidden="true"; the text beside them carries the meaning. Real images get alt text that describes the composition, not the filename.
  • Contrast floors are load-bearingText sits at 4.5:1 or better on its ground, and a control’s boundary at 3:1. That is why the sage has one value for light grounds and one for charcoal: the light sage reads 2.63:1 on charcoal, where the sage on dark reaches 7.47:1. 01

How it’s enforced

A design system is only as true as the last time someone checked it. Twelve scripts check this one, reading the four specs as rules and every rendered page as evidence, after every merge across all twenty-seven pages.

In about a second, with nothing installed

  • deployable.mjsThe tree can ship: no tracked symlink or ignored file that would break the deploy.
  • tokens.mjsEvery token the specs and this page name exists in the CSS, and every swatch here resolves to a declared value.
  • counts.mjsEvery number the docs assert is recounted from the files on disk, including the page count in the sentence above.
  • seo.mjsEach page’s canonical address, share card, and structured data agree with the page, and the sitemap lists every page once.
  • keys.mjsEach article’s key ideas are still its section headings.
  • oxford.mjsEvery list of three or more, in the copy, the metadata, and the specs, has its comma before the “and.”

In a browser, on every page

  • resting.mjsEvery color a reader can see clears its contrast floor at rest, measured by the pixel where text sits on a photograph.
  • states.mjsEvery hover, focus, and script-applied state clears the same floors, including states a page only reaches after a press.
  • typescale.mjsEvery rendered type size is a step on the scale, at four widths from 375 to 1440px.
  • curves.mjsNo border stops partway around a rounded corner.
  • cards.mjsEvery card on a ground has an edge that reads, at 1.2:1 or better.
  • readtime.mjsEvery page that states a read time states the right one: the words a reader meets at 238 a minute, plus a few seconds for each picture.

The Tailwind build is also rebuilt and compared byte for byte. Each check prints the page count before its verdict, because a check that quietly measures less passes while looking at less.

02

Decision log

This is how each part got there: what the audits found, and what changed. The reasons are what stop a rule from being quietly undone.

ColorEighty hand-written values, brought onto the palette

The first color audit found 80 distinct values against 11 tokens, 26 hex and 54 rgba(), and only 21% of color uses going through a token. Most of the rest sat in illustration SVGs or the six Tailwind case studies.

  • A token that was never defined. site-nav.css asked for --color-accent five times. It did not exist, so all five had always rendered through a literal fallback that happened to look right.
  • A retired color that never left. The raw sage was still in nine places, one of them a token with no consumers. Eight became --color-accent-ring, written as color-mix() so it cannot drift again, and the dead token was deleted.
  • Two accessibility failures, both in the lightbox. An 11px hint at 3.62:1 and a close button whose only boundary measured 1.93:1. Both were fixed, and the accessibility rules stopped claiming 4.5:1 everywhere until it was true.
  • Six near-blacks and their near-duplicates collapsed onto black, one scrim token, white, and charcoal. Every off-palette value left is a documented exception.
  • The Tailwind color list, copied six times, became one set of var(--color-*) references: 510 utility replacements, and zero rendered colors changed.

Ranking near-duplicates by luminance got two of them wrong, including a slate blue matched to the sage because the two are equally light. Luminance answers how light a color is, which is right for contrast and wrong for whether two colors are the same.

ColorFour sages became three

The sage on light was two values: one for text, and a lighter one for fills. Nobody had checked: the primary button had used the text step all along, because warm type on the lighter sage measured 4.12:1, under the text floor.

Merging the two found the one place a straight swap failed: the focus ring on charcoal, where the text sage reads 2.63:1. The ring became a token each ground sets, and on charcoal it went from 3.40:1 to 7.47:1.

ButtonsA hover nobody could see

The state grid on this page showed it the day it was built: none of the three buttons changed visibly under a pointer.

  • The primary mixed the sage 90% with transparent, letting the ground through, so it moved 1.25:1 from rest. On white its warm label fell to 4.24:1, under the text floor, and no check saw it, because a color-mix() with transparency is a color the contrast checks cannot composite. It takes --color-accent-hover now: 6.90:1, and a change you can see.
  • The ghost filled charcoal on hover, which on the dark band is no change at all. Every ghost on the site turned out to be the light-ground form, whose hover already inverted, so the dark form takes the dark band’s hover wash.
  • The back pill had a transition and no hover rule. It takes the light-ground ghost’s, so the two outline buttons answer a pointer the same way.

The same pass found this page documenting the buttons on the wrong ground: primary and ghost live on light grounds, and on the dark band the primary turns warm.

AlphaTwenty alphas onto seven

Warm carried fourteen alphas on charcoal, and charcoal six more. Both collapsed onto the seven in the Alpha table. 39 elements changed color, and every one still passes AA. The pass also found a surface tint with no token, so the washes exist now.

ElevationNineteen hand-written shadows onto ten tokens

The spec said two tokens covered the site’s shadows. There were 19 hand-written box-shadow values, and five were the same lifted-screen idea written five ways. Fifteen distinct values became ten, named for what is lifting.

--shadow-media was a single 24px halo at 8%, the only thing saying where a cream drawing stopped. It is a ring, a contact shadow, and a tucked drop now, so the blur sits under the figure.

TypeThree rules that were styling nothing

A pass meant to be mechanical, moving nine clamp() expressions onto ramps, found three of the nine dead: overridden, unmatched, or outranked. None of it showed in the stylesheet; each surfaced because the change was measured in a browser.

DisclosureThree patterns became one

The site once folded content three ways, and this page’s toggles drew no mark at all, leaving the browser to draw one. Chrome, Safari, and Firefox each draw it differently. A component with no mark of its own gets the browser’s.

LayoutTwelve breakpoints onto four

Twelve distinct widths described nine boundaries, each chosen for one component rather than the page. Six went. Three of the four that stayed were Tailwind’s, already on the case studies, and 900px earned its place when 1024 was tried and was worse.

SpacingThe measure moved from rem to ch

Figcaptions were capped at the same 40rem as 18px body copy, which is 70 characters at 18px and 115 at 12px. So --measure is 52ch and goes on the text element itself.

MediaOne bezel, and concentric corners

The phone frames were 5, 8, 8, and 10px, and ran inversely to the device: the largest mockup had the thinnest frame. Three screens’ corners were off concentric. One --bezel and a derived inner radius fixed both.
