# Say When: the creative brief

Say When is a self-initiated, fictional coffee brand, made in October 2026 as
the flagship of the site's repositioning. There is no client, no roaster, no
research study, no print run and no sale behind it. This document records
how the idea was chosen; `coffee-identity-rationale.md` records how it was
drawn, and `asset-manifest.md` records how every file was made and what a
real production would still need.

## What the category looks like, honestly

The "research" is a designer reading shelves and sites, not a study. Stated
as signals, which is all they are:

- Specialty coffee packaging describes itself in a second language: process
  (washed, natural), elevation (masl), varietal, farm and a tasting note
  vocabulary borrowed from wine. It is accurate and it is addressed to people
  who already drink this way.
- The last decade's packaging swung to loud, dense, maximal illustration; the
  more recent pieces argue for quieter, more honest design. That is a trend
  piece's observation, not data, and it was taken as permission rather than
  as proof.
- The big Minnesota coffee brand already owns the northwoods, adventure and
  "stay awake" energy. Anything new here has to avoid seize-the-day, fuel and
  the forest.
- Online, the specialty shop's discovery pattern is a filter by origin,
  process and roast level, which assumes the vocabulary the shelf assumes.

## The hypothesis

Specialty coffee can feel exclusive, technical or intimidating, so there may
be room for a sophisticated brand built on everyday rituals, personality,
discovery and enjoyment without pretension. The brief's own words, taken as a
starting point rather than a conclusion.

## Three territories

### 1. The small hours

- **Premise.** Coffee as the private ritual before the day: the twenty quiet
  minutes nobody else is up for.
- **Audience.** People who guard that time and buy a slightly better bag for
  it.
- **Insight.** The first cup is the one people are most sentimental about and
  most particular about.
- **Emotional proposition.** Calm, before anything has happened.
- **Personality.** Quiet, warm, a little reverent.
- **Naming possibilities.** Small Hours, Early Light, Before.
- **Tone.** Soft, present tense, short sentences.
- **Visual references and rationale.** Low light, pale grounds, thin serifs;
  the look of a good hotel's breakfast card.
- **Campaign opportunity.** "The part of the day that is yours."
- **Digital opportunity.** A subscription timed to the week's mornings.
- **Why it lost.** The calm-morning coffee brand already exists several times
  over, and it tells people how to feel. It is one moment in a day the brand
  wanted all of.

### 2. Plain speech

- **Premise.** A brand that writes the bag in the customer's words: "tastes
  like toast with marmalade" instead of "notes of citrus and honey, 1,950
  masl."
- **Audience.** People who find tasting notes a test they did not study for.
- **Insight.** The vocabulary is the barrier; the coffee was never the
  problem.
- **Emotional proposition.** Relief. Nobody is testing you.
- **Personality.** Dry, direct, helpful.
- **Naming possibilities.** Plainly, In Other Words, Say So.
- **Tone.** The translation as the joke: the jargon crossed out, the plain
  line under it.
- **Visual references and rationale.** Typographic, editorial, one colour per
  product; the look of a well-set dictionary page.
- **Campaign opportunity.** "Jargon, translated."
- **Digital opportunity.** A finder that asks plain questions.
- **Why it half won.** The translation is exactly right and it is kept, on
  the bag and on the shop's front page. On its own it is a feature rather
  than a feeling: it tells you what the brand will not do and nothing about
  what it is like to be its customer.

### 3. Say when

- **Premise.** The pour. Someone fills your cup and you say one word, and it
  stops where you wanted. The brand is the one pouring.
- **Audience.** Anyone who has ever said it: people who like good coffee and
  do not want to be examined on it.
- **Insight.** The most generous thing a person does with coffee is pour it
  for someone else, and the category offers expertise everywhere and
  hospitality nowhere.
- **Emotional proposition.** Being met where you are. Everyone's when is
  different.
- **Personality.** Warm, plain, a little dry. Never cute, never caffeinated.
- **Naming possibilities.** Say When (the territory names itself), Pour,
  When.
- **Tone.** Describes, never instructs. Specific about taste, vague about
  virtue. One dry joke a page, no exclamation marks.
- **Visual references and rationale.** A line at the level you asked for:
  the one shape that can be the mark, the packaging system and the campaign.
  Paper and ink, three product grounds that rise through the day.
- **Campaign opportunity.** "Everyone has a when": the line drawn under the
  moments people already have.
- **Digital opportunity.** A shop whose discovery is three plain questions
  (when, how, how strong) rather than a filter by origin, and a cart that
  says when.
- **Why it won.** It is a gesture rather than a position, it holds the
  translation idea inside it, it names itself, and it gives the whole system
  one thing to draw. It is also the territory with the best digital product
  in it: the finder is the pour as an interface.

## The choice, and what was ruled out

Judged on originality, visual potential, coherence and support for a
compelling digital product, the third territory wins on all four. The first
is familiar; the second is a device; the third is an idea with a device
inside it.

Ruled out on the way, whatever the territory: caffeine-as-fuel and
seize-the-day copy; northwoods, cabins and adventure imagery; farm, burlap
and origin romance; the cafe-chalkboard hand; an elaborate logo system where
one mark would do.

## Positioning

- **Positioning statement.** For people who like good coffee and do not want
  to be examined on it, Say When is the specialty roaster that describes its
  coffee the way you would and asks three plain questions to find yours,
  because everyone's when is different.
- **Audience.** Grown-ups who buy whole bean or ground for a press, drink two
  or three cups a day, and have never looked up what masl means.
- **Brand promise.** We meet you where you are, and we never make you feel
  tested.
- **Brand story.** Say when is what you say when someone pours for you. The
  coffee stops where you wanted it, and nobody tells you what you should have
  asked for. We named our three roasts for the moments they are for, we wrote
  the bags in the words you already use, and the line on every one is the
  level in the cup.
- **Tagline.** Good coffee, in your own words.
- **Campaign line.** Everyone has a when.
- **Campaign messaging.** 6:40, before the house wakes. The 2 p.m. you will
  get through. The one you make for someone else.

## Tone of voice guidelines

- Describe; do not instruct. "Tastes like toast with marmalade," never "you
  will love the bright notes."
- Be specific about taste and vague about virtue. No "crafted," no "curated,"
  no "artisanal."
- Plain words, sentence case, no capitals for emphasis, no exclamation marks.
- One dry joke a page. ("Dark chocolate, and you will still sleep.")
- Say what is pretend. On this prototype, every price is a placeholder and
  every page says so first.

## The products

Three roasts, named for moments rather than origins, and each one differs
from the others in four things: ground, the height of its line, its vessel,
and its words.

| | First Pour | Second Pour | Last Pour |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| When | The morning | The middle of the day | The evening, decaf |
| Tastes like | Toast with marmalade | Caramel and a walnut | Dark chocolate and a quiet house |
| Roast | Light | Medium | Medium-dark |
| In the concept | Washed Ethiopian and Colombian | Natural Brazil with a washed Guatemalan | Water-process Colombian decaf |
| Ground | Butter | Clay | Plum |
| The line | Low | Halfway | High |
| The vessel | A mug | A tumbler | A small cup on a saucer |

Plus The Taster (three four-ounce bags), The Gift (a bag, a cup, a card) and
a kraft shipper. The sourcing is plausible and invented; nobody has roasted
any of it.

## Naming

Five names were judged on memorability, personality, relevance, visual
potential and obvious conflicts. Plainly: says the method, reads as a
software product, already in use as one. Second Cup: the right idea, a
well-known Canadian chain. Company: warm and too general to own. Kettle &
Kin: hospitable, and the shape of a dozen homeware brands. Say When: a thing
people already say, in the moment the brand is about, with a gesture and a
line built in.

**No legal clearance is claimed.** "Say When" is a common phrase and cafes,
bars and products may well use it. A preliminary web search was not run
from the environment this was built in; a real launch would begin with a
trademark search in the relevant classes and a domain check, and the name
would change if either came back crowded.
