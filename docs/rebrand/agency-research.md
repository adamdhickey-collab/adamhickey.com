# What the benchmark work teaches

A craft benchmark, read on 2026-10-26: four pieces of work on
collemcvoy.com (La-Z-Boy, Contented Hen, the Minnesota Star Tribune,
Frank's RedHot), the Caribou Coffee relaunch with 10 Thousand Design, and
the coffee-packaging trend writing of the last two years. None of it is
imitated, and the coffee concept on this site is not an assignment for
anyone. The point was to set the depth, breadth and coherence the flagship
had to reach, and to see how strategy becomes visible work.

## The lessons

1. **One emotional idea shapes everything.** La-Z-Boy's platform (comfort as
   your own "ahhh" place) decides the logotype's curves, the warm palette,
   the patterns cut from the logo, the photography of four kinds of
   comfort, the voice, even a scent, and the campaigns under one line. The
   identity is the idea, applied; nothing is decorated after the fact.
2. **A system can stretch across 80 variants and still be one thing.**
   Contented Hen's farm-signage badge holds steady while a landscape
   palette doubles as navigation across the SKUs, and the illustration
   honors the people behind the product without putting them on display.
   Variation comes from a rule, not from recoloring.
3. **Identity, campaign and the digital product have to connect.** The Star
   Tribune work is strategy, identity, a mascot and the product itself,
   plus a manifesto, out-of-home and a State Fair takeover, shared among
   three studios. The digital experience is part of the brand, not a
   channel for it.
4. **A line can be carried through culture.** Frank's RedHot takes one line
   and spends it in culture-led campaigns rather than in product claims.
5. **Know what the neighbors own.** Caribou's relaunch owns seize-the-day
   energy and the northwoods. A new coffee brand from Minnesota that
   borrows either is a footnote to it.
6. **The trend is away from loudness.** Packaging writing suggests a move
   from maximalist shelf-shouting toward quieter, more honest design. A
   signal rather than data, but it points the same way as the hypothesis
   the flagship started from.

## Who does what

Reading the work for roles rather than for style: a **strategist** finds
the insight and writes the platform; a **brand designer** builds the
identity, the logotype, the palette and the rules; an **art director**
sets the photography, illustration and composition and keeps every piece on
the idea; a **digital designer** carries the system into the site, the
app and the interactive pieces and makes them work on a phone; a
**developer** makes the motion and the behavior real. On the flagship one
person plays all five in turn, and the case study says which decision
belonged to which hat.

## How it applies to Say When

- One idea: the pour, and that everyone's "when" is different. It decides
  the mark, the fill line, the product names, the packaging architecture,
  the campaign and the finder.
- A rule, not a recolor: each bag differs in its ground, the height of its
  line, its vessel and its words, inside one family.
- The storefront is part of the brand: the finder speaks the brand's plain
  language, the cart and checkout keep its tone, and the motion follows
  its principles.
- The line carries beyond the bag: "Everyone has a when" is a campaign
  about moments, not roasts.
- Nothing northwoods, nothing about seizing the day, nothing about caffeine
  as fuel.
