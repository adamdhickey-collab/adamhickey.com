Engagement 03 · Senior capacity, for a while

Embedded Senior Product Design

Give an initiative that has no senior owner one, inside your team rather than beside it, for a defined stretch that ends in a shipped thing or a clean handover.

Best for
An initiative that matters, is funded, and is moving sideways because nobody senior owns its design
Typical length
Three to six months, two to four days a week, priced as reserved weekly capacity
Your team’s involvement
Your standups, your tools, your cadence, and one sponsor who owns what done means
You leave with
The initiative shipped or handed over, the decisions documented, and patterns your team can extend

Before you decide anything

Not sure this is the one? Start with a few sentences.

Tell me about the initiative and where it has stalled, in your own words: what it is meant to ship, who is on it, which decisions are waiting on someone who is not there. No deck, no call to book. I will read it and tell you plainly whether this engagement fits, and if it does not, what would.

If the number that comes back is more than you have, say so. There is usually a smaller version — two days a week rather than four, or a shorter stretch — and it is a different engagement rather than this one discounted.

The situation

There is an initiative that matters, and it needs someone senior to own the design of it end to end. Hiring for it takes months you do not have, and it may not be a permanent role once this is done. Meanwhile it moves sideways: funded, staffed with engineers, and waiting on decisions nobody is placed to make.

Where it has worked

One stretch where a senior owner inside the team was the turn. Every figure is from the case study it sits beside.

Nutrition Incentive Hub · USDA-backed, eight months

Grantees spent more time chasing paperwork than running their programs.

The network ran on old software, spreadsheets and workarounds, and no two of them agreed. For eight months I was the lead product designer inside that effort, from the interviews through the information architecture to one portal and reporting platform that replaced all of it, with a documented design system built in from the start. The component library went to the developers ready to use, so the clarity outlasted the launch.

How it works, step by step

Four steps, each one something you can check off. To show what each produces, they follow one company through, a composite rather than a client: a healthcare provider whose scheduling initiative was funded six months ago and has moved sideways since, because nobody senior owns the design of it.

  1. Agree the initiative and what done means

    One initiative, with a written definition of finished. Embedded work that starts without this becomes general capacity, and general capacity is how a defined stretch turns into an open-ended one.

  2. Join the rhythm you already have

    Your standups, your tools, your review cadence. No parallel process beside your team, and no handover meeting between me and the people building it.

  3. Own the design work end to end

    Research through to the built thing, including the unglamorous middle: the states, the edge cases, the pull-request comments. Ownership means the parts nobody volunteers for.

  4. Leave it maintainable

    Documented decisions, patterns your team can extend, and a deliberate handover. The measure of this engagement is what happens in the three months after it ends.

What it asks of you

  • One sponsor who owns what done means and can say no to what is not it
  • A seat in your standups, your tools, and your repository, from the first week
  • Engineers who will take design in pull requests and conversations, not handoffs
  • A named person to hand over to, agreed at the start rather than found at the end

How I work

The person in your standups is the person doing the design, with no account layer between them and you. A one-person practice runs few engagements at once, so the days you reserve are the days you get, and the stretch has an end date. They are reserved rather than billed back: a quiet week costs what a busy one does, because what you are holding is the room to be there when something important appears. The handover starts in the first month, not the last week, because the measure of the work is what your team can do without me.

This is not the fit when the initiative already has a senior owner and needs more hands beside them, or when the work has no end. If you will still need the role filled next year, that is a hire, and I would rather say so than bill for the wait.
