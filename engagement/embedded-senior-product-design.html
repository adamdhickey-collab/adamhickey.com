The situation

There is an initiative that matters, and it needs someone senior to own the design of it end to end. Hiring for it takes months you do not have, and it may not be a permanent role once this is done. This is that person, for a defined stretch, inside your team rather than beside it.

An embedded engagement in practice: Reducing operational overhead by 80% through a grant-management portal.

How it works, step by step

Four steps, in order, each one something you can check off. No step produces a deliverable for its own sake.

  1. Agree the initiative and what done means

    One initiative, with a written definition of what finished looks like. Embedded work that starts without this becomes general capacity, and general capacity is how a defined stretch turns into an open-ended one.

  2. Join the rhythm you already have

    Your standups, your tools, your review cadence. I am not running a parallel process next to your team, and the point of embedding is that there is no handover meeting between me and the people building it.

  3. Own the design work end to end

    Research through to the built thing, including the unglamorous middle: the states, the edge cases, the pull-request comments. Ownership means the parts nobody volunteers for, not just the parts that present well.

  4. Leave it maintainable

    Documented decisions, patterns your team can extend, and a deliberate handover. The measure of this engagement is what happens in the three months after it ends.

What you leave with

  • The initiative designed and shipped, or handed over mid-flight in a state someone can pick up
  • Decisions documented where your team will find them
  • Patterns your team can extend without me
  • A deliberate handover, not a last day

Working with one person

You talk to the person doing the work

No account layer, no team of juniors briefed second-hand, no rewriting of what you said before it reaches the person designing. You explain the problem once, to the person who will be solving it.

The attention is undivided and it is finite

A one-person practice takes on few engagements at a time, which is the point: yours gets the whole of the attention while it runs. It is also the constraint, and the honest reason to start a conversation earlier than you need to.

Where this is not the right fit

You need capacity spread across many small tickets, or someone to manage a design team. This is one person owning one hard thing.

Shape, scope and cost

Every engagement is scoped to the problem in front of it, so the length and the price come out of a first conversation rather than a menu. That conversation is free and usually short.
