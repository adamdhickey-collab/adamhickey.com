How I work · An initiative with no senior owner

Own the design inside the team, not beside it

Give an initiative that has no senior owner one, inside the team rather than beside it, from the first sketch to a shipped thing or a clean handover.

When this is the job
An initiative that matters, is funded, and is moving sideways because nobody senior owns the design of it

What it asks of the team

What it leaves behind

The situation

There is an initiative that matters, and it needs someone senior to own the design of it end to end. Until someone does, it moves sideways: funded, staffed with engineers, and waiting on decisions nobody is placed to make. The owner it needs sits inside the team, in its standups and its repository, not beside it in a review meeting.

Funded, staffed, and waiting on someone who is not there.

Where it has worked

Two accounts. One is a senior owner inside the team for eight months; the other is what learning the job first did to the design. The figures are the case studies’ own.

Nutrition Incentive Hub · USDA-backed, eight months

Grantees spent more time chasing paperwork than running their programs.

The network ran on old software, spreadsheets and workarounds, and no two of them agreed. For eight months I was the lead product designer inside that effort, from the interviews through the information architecture to one portal and reporting platform that replaced all of it, with a documented design system built in from the start. The component library went to the developers ready to use, so the clarity outlasted the launch.

  • ~10 hrsto process a data request, down from 45 to 55 hrs
  • ~2×the grantees served, without adding analysts

That is the shape of it at full length: one senior owner inside the team from the interviews to the platform, leaving behind something the team could extend without me.

On a dispatch floor at Midwest Couriers the same rule came first: I sat with the dispatchers and learned the job before redesigning the tool, and the map I had assumed was the answer went out when watching them work proved it wrong.

How it goes, step by step

Four steps, each with something to show for it. The example under each one is drawn from the same example company, so the four add up to one story: a healthcare provider whose scheduling initiative was funded six months ago and has moved sideways since, because nobody senior owns the design of it.

  1. Agree the initiative and what done means

    One initiative, with a written definition of finished. Embedded work that starts without this becomes general capacity, and general capacity is how a defined stretch turns into an open-ended one.

    e.g.Done, on one page. The scheduler replaces the phone tree for three clinics, and nothing else.

  2. Join the rhythm you already have

    Your standups, your tools, your review cadence. No parallel process beside your team, and no handover meeting between me and the people building it.

    e.g.No meeting invented for my benefit. Their standups, their board, their release train.

  3. Own the design work end to end

    Research through to the built thing, including the unglamorous middle: the states, the edge cases, the pull-request comments. Ownership means the parts nobody volunteers for.

    e.g.The states nobody volunteers for. The double-booked slot, the clinic that closes early, the patient who cancels twice.

  4. Leave it maintainable

    Documented decisions, patterns your team can extend, and a deliberate handover. The measure of the work is what the team can do in the three months after the initiative ships.

    e.g.Two of their designers can extend it without calling me. The handover started in the first month, not the last week.

What it asks of the team

  • A product owner who owns what done means and can say no to what is not it
  • A seat in your standups, your tools, and your repository, from the first week
  • Engineers who will take design in pull requests and conversations, not handoffs
  • A named person to hand over to, agreed at the start rather than found at the end

Questions people ask

How long does it take?
The life of the initiative. What done means is written down at the start, so the stretch has a shape, and the handover starts in the first month rather than the last week.
Is this something you do alone?
No. I sit in the team’s standups, work in its repository, and take design into pull requests and conversations rather than handoffs. Engineering is in the room from the first sketch, and the people who do the job today are in it before the first sketch.
