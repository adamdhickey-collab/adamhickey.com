About

Adam Hickey is a senior product designer in Minneapolis, Minnesota, with twenty years across visual design, interactive experiences and enterprise software: eight years designing and building brand-led sites and apps at three studios, thirteen years designing enterprise applications and design systems at RBA, and products of his own since.

I started in graphic design. The thing I still enjoy most is the moment a complicated thing turns into something clear, and a clear thing turns into something you want to use.

Studios · 2005–2013I began as a graphic designer and a hybrid interactive designer and developer at ByDesign, LEVEL and Pixel Farm: visually rich websites, interactive experiences, digital campaigns and creative assets for Lund Boats, Fulton Beer, Hazelden and Learn to Live. I drew the site and then built the front end, and whoever shipped it sat close enough to argue with. The studio years.

Enterprise · 2013–2026Then thirteen years at RBA designing complex digital products, enterprise applications and design systems for CBRE, Toro, Banner Bank, International Trucks and a global SAP program. That work widened what I understood about users, systems and consistency: forty applications have to read as one product, and a visual decision repeats on every screen it touches.

What people kept coming back for was the same in both places: a complicated interface made clear, a hierarchy that holds across hundreds of screens, and the details fixed before the software felt unfinished. Some of it I explained in public.

Both at once · 2026–nowSince RBA I’ve designed and built things end to end: a travel brand for my sister, a training app for our dog, a screen-print studio, a book, and a fictional coffee brand made to show the whole arc. Coding agents and image models are tools in that, the way jQuery was; the judgment is mine.

The two halves of the career turned out to be one job: a graphic designer’s eye, applied to software that has to work. What I want next is a senior hands-on design role on one team, remote or hybrid in the Twin Cities.

The test I still use is one question: when the work ships, does it look like someone cared about every part of it, and does it work the way it looks?
